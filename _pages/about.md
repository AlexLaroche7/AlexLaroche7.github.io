I'm a PhD candidate[^funding] in the [Department of Astronomy & Astrophysics](https://www.astro.utoronto.ca/) at the [University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/), working with [Maria Drout](https://www.astro.utoronto.ca/~drout/) and [Josh Speagle](https://joshspeagle.com/). I'm interested in binary interaction: how the exchange of mass, energy and angular momentum between two stars changes their evolution and final fates. Right now I'm focused on the [intermediate-mass helium stars](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade4970) recently discovered in the Magellanic Clouds, which lost their hydrogen envelopes through binary interaction with a companion.

I'm an observer and a data explorer. I use [spectroscopy](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260930384L/abstract) from instruments such as [Magellan/MagE](https://www.lco.cl/technical-documentation/mage/), [VLT/X-shooter](https://www.eso.org/sci/facilities/paranal/instruments/xshooter.html) and [Gemini/GHOST](https://www.gemini.edu/instrumentation/ghost), together with binary evolution modeling, to study small samples of post-interaction binaries in detail. If the TACs are merciful, perhaps [HST](https://www.stsci.edu/hst) or [JWST](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst) soon! I also build data-driven models to find new binaries and measure stellar properties in big datasets, such as [Gaia XP spectra](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979....5L/abstract). These two approaches are complementary: detailed studies provide empirical constraints on the uncertain physics in binary evolution models, while surveys present an opportunity to discover new systems and characterize full(er) populations. More details are on my [research](/research/) page.

I grew up in Montreal, Quebec, and graduated from [McGill University](https://www.mcgill.ca/) in 2022 with an Honours BSc in [Physics](https://www.physics.mcgill.ca/). As an undergraduate I worked on dark matter and strong lensing with [Jo Bovy](https://astro.utoronto.ca/~bovy/) at UofT, and on 21cm cosmology in [Adrian Liu's](http://www.physics.mcgill.ca/~acliu/) and [Jonathan Sievers'](https://www.physics.mcgill.ca/~sievers/) groups at McGill.
